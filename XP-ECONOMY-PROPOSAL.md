## 2. What "75 Hard" means in Forge — and why it matters for the benchmark

You asked whether the reference uses the original two-workout requirements.
**It does not.** Forge's `75_day_discipline_challenge` is a modified, lighter
challenge:

| | Canonical 75 Hard | Forge standard variant | Forge hard variant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workouts | **2 × 45 min, one outdoors** | 1 × 45 min | 1 × 45 min + "additional movement or conditioning" |
| Diet | strict, no cheat meals, **no alcohol** | "follow diet / eat clean" | same |
| Water | 1 US gallon | "hit water goal" (user-set) | same |
| Reading | 10 pages non-fiction | 10 pages | 10 pages |
| Progress photo | yes | yes | yes |
| Also required | — | mental training, sleep target, quote/reflection | + the above |
| On a miss | **restart at day 1** | passing score 80%, keystone 70% | same |

So Forge's standard variant is **one workout, no outdoor requirement, no
alcohol clause, and no restart rule** — roughly **70 minutes/day** of active
work. The hard variant is the closest analogue to canonical 75 Hard, and even
it only adds "movement or conditioning", not a second full workout.

There is also an internal contradiction: the template's `overall_difficulty` is
`'Hard'` while its **standard variant's own `difficulty` field says `'medium'`**.
The two disagree today.

**This is a decision for you (§13.2).** The benchmark has to anchor to something
specific:

- **Option 1 (assumed throughout this document):** anchor to Forge's **standard
  variant as it exists**, at the `hard` tier (200/day). The 365-day curve then
  describes what a Forge user actually does when they tap 75-Day Discipline.
- **Option 2:** make the standard variant match canonical 75 Hard (two
  workouts, outdoor session, no-alcohol, restart-on-miss) and keep it at `hard`.
  The benchmark stays 365 days but the challenge gets materially harder.
- **Option 3:** add canonical 75 Hard as a separate `elite` configuration
  (220/day) and anchor the 365-day benchmark to *it*. Forge's existing standard
  variant then correctly takes ~405 days, because it is easier.

I recommend **Option 1** for now — it keeps the benchmark attached to real user
behaviour — with Option 3 as a later addition if you want a canonical mode.

**LOCK IN is under-rated.** At 135 stated minutes a day it demands roughly

template's tier. This also resolves the 75 Hard template-vs-variant

## 5. Revised proposal

### 5.1 The model

> **Difficulty sets a daily XP budget. Tasks divide that budget by weight.**

Task count stops driving XP, which is what makes §8's profile disparity
structurally impossible rather than merely patched.

```
dailyBudget = TIER[effortTier] × MODE[selectedMode]
taskXP[i]   = dailyBudget × weight[i] / Σweight    (integer, sums exactly)
```

### 5.2 Constants — one versioned module

```js
export const XP_ECONOMY = {
  version: 2,

  tiers: { easy: 120, medium: 160, medium_hard: 180, hard: 200, elite: 220 },
  modes: { beginner: 0.85, standard: 1.00, hard: 1.08 },   // hard was 1.12

  perfectDayRate: 0.15,
  faithRate:       0.05,
  recoveryDayRate: 0.50,      // replaces the day's task XP, not added to it

  // Linear in duration, so worth the same per day at every length.
  completionRate: 0.20,

  // Milestones stay, but an attempt's TOTAL streak XP is capped at a flat
  // per-day rate. The cap is what makes short restarts worthless.
```

| Rule | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Whole day missed | −25 flat | **0.50 × daily budget** |

| Recovery-day multiplier | ×0.3 | ×0.3 (unchanged) |

| medium_hard | +207 | −90 | 43% |

A missed day costs a consistent **43%** of what the day would have earned, at
every difficulty — up from 16%, and never more than the day was worth, so no

(→ −6,490 at display scale). Era-2 penalty rates never apply to it.

### 10.3 Design rules

1. **Historical rates, always.** Each archive stores its economy version; its
   breakdown recomputes under *that* version. An era-1 attempt is never
   revalued at era-2 rates.
2. **Earned and penalties stay separate**, as the ledger already keeps them.
3. **The totals reconcile** — earned − penalties + floor cancellations =
   Current XP. Already enforced in XP History; extended here.
4. **Each period expands** to show its tasks, perfect-day bonuses, streaks,
   completion bonus, capped optional XP and deductions, with the specific days
   that were charged.
5. **Era labels are visible**, so a large era-1 figure beside a smaller era-2
   one is explained rather than confusing.
6. Built on the existing `XPHistory.jsx` and `xpLedger.js` — extended, not
   replaced.

## 11. Migration — Option A, reversible and auditable

### 11.1 Nothing stored is rewritten

Your instruction was a reversible, auditable conversion layer rather than a
destructive rewrite. That is now the design:

- **Stored values stay exactly as written.** `archive.xpEarned` stays 8,245-era
  numbers. `xpLedger.reconciled.amount` stays 649. Nothing is multiplied on disk.
- **A conversion layer applies at read time**, keyed by the record's era:
  `displayXP = rawXP × SCALE[era]`, where `SCALE = { 1: 10, 2: 1 }`.
- **Reversible by definition** — delete the scale table and every stored number
  still means what it meant. No data is lost because no data is changed.
- **Auditable** — the XP Breakdown shows each period's era and both figures on
  request, so any displayed number can be traced to the record behind it.

This is strictly better than the in-place multiply I proposed in revision 1: it
cannot corrupt an archive, it needs no backfill of historical rows, and a
future era-3 is another table entry rather than another migration.

### 11.2 Attempts in progress keep their rules

The economy version is stamped on the attempt descriptor at **start**, not read
from global config at compute time. An attempt begun under era 1 computes under
era 1 until it is archived — mid-attempt revaluation is impossible by
construction, not by policy. Regression test: start an attempt, change the
global era, assert the attempt's per-day XP is unchanged.

### 11.3 Floors, ledger and forgiveness scale exactly

Verified across the paths that could hide debt:

| case | earned | penalty | floor | ×1 shown | ×10 shown | exact? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before reconciliation | 8,245 | 0 | 7,500 | 8,245 | 82,450 | ✓ |
| after reconciliation | 8,245 | 649 | 7,500 | 7,596 | 75,960 | ✓ |
| charge exceeds headroom | 7,600 | 649 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 75,000 | ✓ |
| exactly at the floor | 7,500 | 100 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 75,000 | ✓ |
| below floor, fully forgiven | 7,000 | 500 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 75,000 | ✓ |

`shown(×10) = 10 × shown(×1)` in every case. Floors, forgiveness and the ledger
are all linear, so **the conversion cannot change anyone's rank or create
hidden debt** — which was the explicit requirement. The forgiveness path matters
most: it is the one that could have produced invisible debt, and it scales
exactly like the rest.
